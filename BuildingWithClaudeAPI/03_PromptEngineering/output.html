# One-Day Meal Plan for Marathon Training Recovery

Here is a one-day meal plan for an athlete aiming to support marathon training recovery with high carbohydrate intake and proper meal timing:

* **Calorie Target:** Approximately 3,200 calories
* **Macronutrient Breakdown:** Protein (110g), Fat (80g), Carbs (440g)

## Meal Plan:

* **Breakfast (7:00 AM):** Oatmeal (100g dry weight) with banana (150g), dried raisins (40g), and sunflower seeds (20g). Whole milk (240ml).
  * Protein: 16g, Fat: 12g, Carbs: 85g

* **Pre-Long Run Snack (9:30 AM - 1.5 hours before run):** White bread (80g) with honey (30g) and jam (25g). Orange juice (250ml).
  * Protein: 5g, Fat: 3g, Carbs: 80g

* **Post-Long Run Recovery (2:00 PM - within 30 minutes after run):** Chocolate milk (500ml) with a banana (120g) and rice cakes (50g) with jam (20g).
  * Protein: 18g, Fat: 8g, Carbs: 120g

* **Lunch (3:30 PM):** Vegetarian pasta (120g dry weight) with marinara sauce (200g), chickpeas (150g canned), zucchini (100g), and olive oil (15ml). Whole wheat bread (60g).
  * Protein: 22g, Fat: 12g, Carbs: 95g

* **Afternoon Snack (5:30 PM):** Greek yogurt (200g, plain) with granola (50g), blueberries (100g), and honey (15g).
  * Protein: 18g, Fat: 8g, Carbs: 55g

* **Dinner (7:30 PM):** Lentil and vegetable curry (300g) with basmati rice (100g cooked), spinach (80g), and coconut milk (100ml). Naan bread (80g).
  * Protein: 22g, Fat: 20g, Carbs: 95g

* **Evening Snack (9:30 PM):** Rice pudding (200g) made with milk and raisins.
  * Protein: 9g, Fat: 7g, Carbs: 60g

---

## Key Nutritional Considerations:

This meal plan emphasizes **high carbohydrate intake** (440g total, approximately 6.5g/kg) for optimal glycogen replenishment during marathon training recovery. The strategic timing of meals around your long run maximizes performance and recovery:

- **Pre-run fueling** provides easily digestible carbs to sustain energy
- **Post-run recovery meal** within 30 minutes replenishes glycogen and supports muscle repair
- **Plant-based proteins** from legumes (lentils, chickpeas), dairy (milk, yogurt), and whole grains support muscle adaptation
- **All foods are nut-free and vegetarian**, meeting your dietary requirements

**Hydration reminder:** Drink water throughout the day, especially before, during, and after your long run. Aim for 500-750ml of water per hour during running.
